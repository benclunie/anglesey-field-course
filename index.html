Choosing a reference and recording an identification
  • Choose guides for the habitat and season. In autumn or winter, flower photographs and breeding-seabird sheets have limits; use vegetative plant characters and bird structure as well as colour.
  • Check more than one diagnostic feature. Record the date, place, habitat, size and useful photographs. Keep uncertain records at genus or group level, and ask a tutor to review them.
  • A spotter sheet is a shortlist, not a species-richness key. Do not count broad groups such as coral weeds or flat periwinkles as if they were resolved species.

Core broad rocky-shore guide

An introduction to recording rocky shore life in Northern Ireland

Christine C. Morrow / CEDaR, National Museums Northern Ireland (2020)

Publisher PDF · 98 pages · 11.9 MB

Broad illustrated field reference with diagnostic characters, similar species, sizes and habitats. Covers lichens, seaweeds, sponges, anemones and hydroids, polychaetes, crustaceans, molluscs, echinoderms, sea squirts and rockpool fish. Clear one-species-per-page layout.

Northern Ireland selection, not a complete Anglesey checklist. Statements about absence of similar species or likely local occurrence refer to Northern Ireland; check southern or unfamiliar finds in MarLIN. Names follow the 2020 guide. Some groups need microscopy for confirmation.
